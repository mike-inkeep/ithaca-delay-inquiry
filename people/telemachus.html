Telemachus

Prince of Ithaca. Son of Odysseus and Penelope. The Inquiry's unpaid precursor.

"My mother says I am his son; for myself, I do not know. No one truly knows his own begetting."
— Telemachus, opening his own investigation with better source discipline than the entire tradition

Inquiry profile

Field Value
Age at father's departure Infant
Age at father's return 20

Own investigative journey Pylos and Sparta (Books 1–4, the "Telemachy")

The Telemachy, audited

At twenty, with the household consuming itself (The Home Renovation Avoidance Hypothesis describes the environment), Telemachus did what no god had bothered to: opened a missing-persons file. He sailed to Pylos and Sparta, interviewed the two most senior surviving commanders of the Troy expedition, and collected the war's oral history plus one hard datum — Menelaus's report, sourced from a shape-shifting sea god, that the subject was alive, on an island, "detained by a nymph."

The Committee notes with professional envy that Telemachus obtained in weeks, with one borrowed ship and no budget, the single most accurate status report of the entire decade. It has been entered as the Inquiry's benchmark for what diligence looks like. It also — the Committee notes with less envy — triggered a suitor assassination plot, because on Ithaca even fact-finding was hazardous.

Structural role in the delay

The theories dispute what the son's existence means for the file:

  • The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination files him as the strongest pull home — and therefore, by its own grim logic, part of what made arriving heavy: a man returns to a son he cannot recognize and a fatherhood already twenty years in arrears.
  • The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation files him as the ticking clock that made "I'll decide next year" fiscally indefensible by year seven.
  • The Committee files him as the only party who behaved throughout as if time was real — a distinction shared, on the domestic side, with Penelope.

The reunion

The record's reunion scene — in a swineherd's hut, the father un-disguised by Athena mid-conversation — contains the son's full character: he first refused to believe it, on evidentiary grounds ("you were an old beggar a moment ago; that is not how humans work"), and demanded verification. Twenty years of absent-father mythology, and the boy's instinct was source criticism. The Committee has a favorite witness. It is him.
