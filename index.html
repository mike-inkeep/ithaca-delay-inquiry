About This Inquiry

The Committee for the Audit of Heroic Itineraries was convened to answer one question:

Why did a decorated veteran with a fast ship, a competent crew, and a wife he claimed to miss take ten years to complete a three-week sail?

We are not the first to ask. We are merely the first to ask with spreadsheets.

What this inquiry is not

  • It is not a character assassination of Odysseus. The record accomplishes that on its own.
  • It is not a theological review. Where gods appear in the evidence, we treat them as uncooperative witnesses, not explanations. "Poseidon was angry" is a weather report, not an itinerary.
  • It is not finished. Wikis never are. See the open items on the front page.

Sources and their problems

The Inquiry rests on three classes of source, in descending order of reliability:

Class Description Reliability
A — Physical Ship logs, invoices, loom fragments, one (1) olive-tree bed High
B — Third-party testimony Penelope, Telemachus, Circe, the Phaeacian court stenographer Mixed
C — The subject's own account Books 9–12, as performed for a paying audience See below

Class C deserves special comment. The bulk of the traditional narrative — Lotus-Eaters through Calypso — comes from a single after-dinner performance delivered by the subject himself, to strangers, in exchange for gifts and a boat ride home (The Phaeacian Speaking Tour). No court on earth admits this as evidence. We admit it as entertainment with coordinates.

The scoring rubric

Each theory in the Theory Catalog receives a Committee Verdict scored on four axes, 0–5:

Axis Question it answers
Textual support Can you point at a line in the record, or are you pointing at vibes?
Days explained How much of the ten years does the theory actually absorb?
Motive plausibility Would a real person do this? (Note: heroes are only barely real people.)
Survives Penelope Could the theory be stated, out loud, to Penelope, and would the theorist survive?

The composite grade is the minimum of the four scores, because a theory is only as strong as its weakest excuse. This is known as the Weakest Excuse Principle and it is the Committee's sole methodological innovation.

The unaccounted-days problem

Summing every delay the subject admits to still leaves a gap — days no monster, goddess, or storm will claim. The current estimate is 43 days (see Delay Attribution for the arithmetic). Theories that absorb unaccounted days score bonus consideration. Theories that create additional unaccounted days (see The Underworld Networking Summit) are asked to sit down.

Editorial standards

  1. Every claim links to its source page. Unlinked claims are marked for citation and quietly resented.
  2. Broken links are permitted only for documents that are genuinely lost, e.g. the Redacted Testimony of Eurylochus.
  3. Speculation is welcome but must be labelled ("The Committee speculates…"), plausible ("…that the goddess had a sales quota…"), and actionable ("…and recommends checking Ogygia's land registry.").
  4. All dates are given relative to the Fall of Troy (FoT). The wiki uses FoT+0 through FoT+3650. See the Timeline of the Ten Years.

The 43 days

The residue survives every audit pass. It is not on Ogygia (Calypso's count is exact to the day — her paperwork, as usual, embarrasses everyone). It is not weather (Poseidon's storms are itemized and bounded). It is distributed, in slivers of one to four days, across departure mornings: the day after the decision to leave, when the record shows the fleet still in harbor.

Claims on the residue:

  • The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination — claims all 43 as "Tuesdays": the manufactured frictions of a reluctant traveler. Currently the only claimant.
  • The Strait Insurance Dispute — offered to absorb 30 into "tribunal scheduling"; the Committee requested minutes; the theory requested an extension.
  • The official account — declined to claim them, twice, which the Committee finds more informative than any claim.

Methodological note

Attribution is not exculpation. That Calypso claims 2,555 days establishes where the subject was, not why. The why lives in the Theory Catalog, and the Committee's scoring of the why lives on each theory's verdict table (rubric). This page settles only the where the time went question — the one question, it turns out, with a clean answer.

Reading the graph

  • The two thick nodes are the case. Aeaea (1 year) and Ogygia (7 years) between them absorb 80% of the decade — see Delay Attribution for the wedge chart. Every hazard node on the graph combined is a rounding error by comparison (the timeline's finding).

What the shape shows

  1. The monsters are a rounding error. Every famous hazard of the tradition — cyclops, sirens, Scylla, the lot — fits inside 7% of the decade. The remaining 93% had catering.
  2. The two hospitality blocks are load-bearing. Remove Ogygia and Aeaea and the journey is thirteen months — long, but explicable by weather and war-weariness alone. The Inquiry exists because of two addresses.
  3. The finish was instantaneous. Once the subject reached a functioning travel desk (Scheria), home was one night away. The last 0.1% of the timeline covers the only part where someone else set the schedule.

Glossary

Recurring terms of art. Contributors: keep entries short, link generously, and resist the urge to editorialize (the Committee will do that for you).

Nostos

Greek: "homecoming." The genre convention that a hero eventually returns home. The entire premise of the subject's decade. Notable for being, in this case, aggressively deferred — see The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination for the argument that nostos was the problem, not the goal.

Xenia

Greek: "guest-friendship." The sacred host–guest relationship. In practice, the mechanism by which every host in the Mediterranean was obligated to feed, house, and entertain Odysseus indefinitely, and by which he was obligated to accept. The Committee notes that xenia has no maximum duration clause, an omission exploited on Ogygia (7 years), Aeaea (1 year), and Scheria (3 days, but the catering was exceptional). See The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation and The Aeaean Culinary Residency.

Unaccounted days

Days of the ten-year itinerary that no monster, deity, storm, or documented layover will claim. Current Committee estimate: 43. The central mystery of the Inquiry. Full arithmetic at Delay Attribution.

FoT (Fall of Troy) dating

All Inquiry dates are relative to the Fall of Troy: FoT+0 (the horse) through FoT+3650 (the reunion). The Timeline of the Ten Years renders the whole span. Contributors converting from "years wandering" should note that the subject rounds down when describing delays and up when describing hardships.

The Weakest Excuse Principle

The Committee's scoring rule: a theory's composite grade is the minimum of its four axis scores, because a theory is only as strong as its weakest excuse. Defined in About This Inquiry#the-scoring-rubric.

Polytropos

Greek: "of many turns." The epithet the tradition applies to Odysseus, usually translated "wily" or "much-travelled." The Committee's preferred translation: "evasive, with mileage."

The Loom Question

Whether Penelope's weaving-and-unweaving of the shroud was (a) a delaying tactic against the suitors, (b) a coded message network, or (c) load-bearing for the renovation dispute described in The Home Renovation Avoidance Hypothesis. The Committee declines to rule while Penelope retains counsel.

"Nobody" (Οὖτις)

The alias the subject gave Polyphemus, later described in every port as "the cleverest thing anyone has ever said." See The 'Nobody' Personal Brand Crisis for the argument that the subsequent un-aliasing — shouting his real name, address, and father's name across open water — was the single most expensive PR decision in maritime history.

Committee

The Committee for the Audit of Heroic Itineraries. Convened after the bard stopped taking questions; see About This Inquiry. The Committee has no chairman, because every candidate keeps delaying the vote, which the Committee acknowledges is thematically appropriate.

The Ithaca Delay Inquiry

"Tell me, O Muse, of that ingenious hero who travelled far and wide — but mostly, for reasons this inquiry will examine, not toward home."
— Invocation, as amended by the Committee

The facts are not in dispute. Troy fell. The sail from the Troad to Ithaca is, by the most pessimistic ancient reckoning, about three weeks in fair weather. Odysseus took ten years — a schedule overrun of roughly 17,000%, unmatched in the ancient world until the invention of the public infrastructure project.

The official explanation — divine wrath, sea monsters, bad luck — was supplied by the traveller himself, an unemployed veteran described by his own patron goddess as "the best liar of your generation." This wiki collects, cross-references, and grades the alternative explanations.

Leading theories

Ranked by the Committee's official scoring rubric (explained here):

  2. The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination — the meta-theory: every delay is one man avoiding one conversation.
  3. The Aeaean Culinary Residency — a year is a normal length for a tasting-menu residency; the pig thing was a menu misprint.
  4. The Home Renovation Avoidance Hypothesis — 108 suitors in the house means 108 opinions about the kitchen backsplash.
  5. The 'Nobody' Personal Brand Crisis — you can't go home until the news cycle about the cyclops forgets your name. He made sure it couldn't.
  6. The Lotus-Eaters Wellness Retreat — "briefly" is doing a lot of work in the official account.
  7. The Aeolus Baggage Incident — the only theory where the delay is genuinely the crew's fault, which is why Odysseus likes it so much.
  8. The Underworld Networking Summit — nobody attends a necromancy conference for just one day.
  9. The Sirens' Encore Clause — he demanded to be tied to the mast. Nobody demands front-row seats to something they hate.
  10. The Strait Insurance Dispute — six crewmen lost to Scylla triggered a maritime liability review that took months to adjudicate.
  11. The Thrinacian Catering Invoice — the cattle of the Sun were never eaten; they were expensed, and the paperwork was cursed.
  12. The Phaeacian Speaking Tour — the only audience that ever paid him in boats. Of course he stretched the material.

The vendor's perspective

Aeolus is the Inquiry's cleanest commercial actor. He hosted generously, asked only for Troy stories in payment (a currency the subject mints personally — see The Phaeacian Speaking Tour), and shipped a turnkey weather solution requiring exactly one operating instruction, which he communicated to the client, who communicated it to no one (the information-design finding).

When the fleet blew back to his shore nine days later — the product opened, the delivery squandered — Aeolus performed the ancient world's fastest root-cause analysis: a man to whom this happens is hated by the gods, and gods' enemies are not serviceable accounts. He voided the relationship on the spot. The Committee's procurement analysts call this "the correct read, reached by the wrong method, in record time."

Why his file matters

  • He calibrates hospitality. One month of feasting, and then — uniquely among the record's hosts — he let them leave on schedule, with logistics support. Aeolia is the control case proving that a comfortable venue does not, by itself, generate a seven-year overrun. The overruns needed a second ingredient. See The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation for the Committee's view on what.
  • He proves the road home was real. Nine days, Ithaca in sight, shore fires visible. Every theory that paints the return as impossible must first explain the day it nearly happened — and the sleep, and the bag, and the silence about the bag.

Assessment

As a witness: unavailable by design (the island floats; process servers return seasick). As evidence: his product's performance window is the Inquiry's most valuable dataset — a controlled experiment in what the journey took when someone competent handled the weather: nine days. The remaining 3,641 are the subject of this wiki.

The seven-year question

Calypso's file contains the Inquiry's central asymmetry:

  • Her account: she loved him, she kept him, she was ordered by Olympus to release him, and she complied under protest — with, she notes, better severance than any god ever offered a mortal.
  • His account: captivity, nightly weeping on the shore, an unwilling bed.
  • The record's account: no walls, no guards, raft-grade timber on-site for seven years, and an exit that took four days once anyone decided to exit.

The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation resolves the asymmetry commercially: an open-ended pitch, indefinitely deferred. The venue's significance

Scheria is where the Inquiry's source problem was manufactured. Everything uncorroborated in the tradition — every duration, every monster, every year of weeping — was first performed here, to a paying audience, by candlelight, between courses (source classes). The stenography was excellent. The fact-checking was not attempted. One does not fact-check a keynote whose speaker is crying.

The one-night problem

Scheria's ships gave the Inquiry its most brutal datum: from this island, home was one night's sail. The Phaeacians loaded the sleeping subject aboard at dusk and set him — plus treasure — on an Ithacan beach by dawn, without waking him (the arrival).

The Committee states the implication once, flatly: for ten years, the distance was never the problem. The problem was reaching the decision to cross it — a formulation every file in the Theory Catalog claims as supporting evidence, which is how the Committee knows it's true.

Epilogue: the venue paid for it

Poseidon, denied his preferred outcome, turned the returning ferry to stone within sight of Scheria's harbor. The Phaeacians' response — a policy review on offering rides to strangers — is the record's final hospitality audit, and the reason the Committee's travel-desk motto is "be the last client before the policy change."

The compliance geometry

Thrinacia is the file's cleanest avoidable catastrophe. The advisory did not say be careful on the island. It said keep the ship away from the island. The subject — outvoted by an exhausted crew, per his account; insufficiently persuasive, per the Committee's — anchored his fleet's sole remaining ship beside the one temptation in the Mediterranean with a divine casualty clause, and then the wind stopped.

Thirty days of the South Wind. Supplies out by day twenty. The rest is The Thrinacian Catering Invoice: the oath, the nap of unrecorded length, the oak-leaf sacrament, the lowing meat, the thunderbolt.

Why the venue matters to the delay file

  • It converts the fleet to a single survivor. Every subsequent delay — the nine days' drift, the seven years on Ogygia — happens because Thrinacia removed the crew, the ship, and every witness. The Theory Catalog's uncorroborated back half begins here.
  • It is the strongest evidence against pure-procrastination readings — the month was involuntary, wind-locked, and miserable — and simultaneously the strongest evidence for the management-failure readings: the anchorage was a choice, the briefing was withheld (the recurring pattern), and the enforcement plan was a nap.
  • The sun sees everything. On this island, of all places, the account cannot be embroidered: the proprietor watched. It is the last stretch of the itinerary with a divine eyewitness, and the Committee treats its facts as anchor-truth for calibrating everything after.

The Aeaean Culinary Residency

"They stayed with her a full year, feasting on abundant meat and sweet wine."
— The official account, in a rare moment of not even trying to hide it

Status: Leading theory. Absorbs 365 of the 3,650 missing days (10%), with witnesses.

The Theory

The year on Aeaea was not enchantment. It was a residency. Circe — a niche practitioner with an isolated venue, a herb garden of professional grade, and a documented interest in transformation — hosted the crew of Ithaca's flagship for a twelve-month engagement of what the record itself describes as daily feasting.

The theory's central claim: nobody was tricked. The crew's own testimony (Class B, unusually consistent) is that at the end of the year the crew had to remind Odysseus it was time to leave. This is not the behavior of prisoners. This is the behavior of a party whose lead guest has been comped into complacency — the pattern that recurs, at 7× the scale, on Ogygia (The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation).

The pig incident

The theory must address the pigs. Its account:

  1. The advance party arrived unannounced, twenty-three men, at lunch service.
  2. They were seated anyway (see xenia) and served the tasting menu.
  3. The menu's first course transformed the diner's relationship with food. In twenty-two documented cases, it transformed slightly more than that.
  4. Eurylochus, who waited outside and ate nothing, was not transformed — which the Committee notes is exactly how a menu works.

Circe has provided notarized menus for the relevant service. The transformation clause appears in the footer, in Linear B, as required by the regulations of the era. Her full statement is on file at her page. The missing corroboration is the Redacted Testimony of Eurylochus, lost since antiquity.

Supporting Evidence

  • The witnesses agree. Uniquely in this inquiry, an entire surviving crew corroborates the duration and the catering. No crew member has ever filed a complaint about the year itself. Several filed complaints about leaving.
  • The exit interview included a full itinerary. Circe provided turn-by-turn sailing directions, monster-by-monster, including which crew would not survive (The Strait Insurance Dispute disputes her liability posture). Practitioners do not provide this level of after-care to prisoners.
  • The return visit. After the Underworld errand (The Underworld Networking Summit), the crew sailed back to Aeaea for one more night. You do not return to your captor for the dessert course. You return for the dessert course.

Problems

  • A year is long, even for tasting menus. The longest documented residency of the era ran a season. The theory requires either an unprecedented engagement or an unadmitted romance, and the record supports — awkwardly — both.
  • Survives-Penelope score: 2. "It was a professional engagement" survives right up until the word "notarized."

The Aeolus Baggage Incident

"They opened the bag, and the winds rushed out, and bore them weeping back the way they had come."
— The official account of the only itinerary failure with a receipt

Status: Confirmed in substance; disputed in blame. Absorbs 37 days, fully documented.

The Theory

This is the Inquiry's control case — the one delay where the mechanics are undisputed and the paperwork survives:

  1. Aeolus, Keeper of the Winds, hosts the fleet for one month (the feasting is in the record; see a pattern forming at The Aeaean Culinary Residency).
  2. On departure, he provides the ancient world's most generous parting gift: every adverse wind, bagged, plus a following westerly. Turn-by-turn weather, in luggage form.
  3. Nine days of perfect sailing follow. Ithaca is visible — close enough to see the shore fires.
  4. The subject, who has personally guarded the bag for nine sleepless days without telling the crew what is in it, falls asleep.
  5. The crew, reasoning that the boss's secret bag is treasure he refuses to share, opens it. The winds ship the fleet back to square one.
  6. Aeolus, asked for a refill, declines with prejudice ("hated by the gods" is a strong exit survey) and the fleet rows home into the Laestrygonian disaster.

Why the Committee loves this case

  • Perfect documentation. A named vendor, a physical artifact, a duration, a failure mode, and a hostile exit interview. Every other file in the Theory Catalog wishes it had this.
  • It calibrates the liar. The subject tells this story against his own crew, and it checks out. This is what a true story looks like in his mouth — which is precisely what makes the seven-year "captivity" on Ogygia look the way it looks (The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation).

The blame dispute

The official account blames the crew. The Committee's audit blames information design:

Decision Made by Assessment
Accept a bag of hazardous winds Odysseus Reasonable
Tell no one what the bag contains Odysseus Catastrophic
Guard it personally for nine days, visibly, like treasure Odysseus Actively advertising the bag
Open the mysterious hoarded treasure while the boss sleeps Crew Wrong, but predictable to anyone who has met a crew

A one-sentence briefing — "the bag is weather, do not open the weather" — closes the incident. The Committee scores the root cause as management, and notes that the subject's leadership style (secrecy first, disclosure during the funeral) recurs at Thrinacia (The Thrinacian Catering Invoice) and the strait (The Strait Insurance Dispute).

Problems

  • It's 37 days. One month of hospitality plus the round trip. The theory is airtight and small — a footnote that thinks it's a thesis.
  • It cuts against the subject only obliquely. He wanted, visibly, to get home here — asleep at the tiller, but pointed the right way. The psychological theories must treat this leg as the exception that proves their rule, and The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination does so with what the Committee can only describe as audible reluctance.

The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination

"Sing in me, Muse, and through me tell the story of that man skilled in all ways of contending — the wanderer."
— The tradition's own job title for the subject. Not "the arriver."

Status: The Committee's reluctant favorite. Claims all 3,650 days, including the 43 nobody else will take.

The Theory

Every other file in the Theory Catalog explains a segment. This file explains the shape.

At Troy, the subject was polytropos ("of many turns") — the indispensable man, the stratagem engine, the one who ended a ten-year war with a wooden horse. The war's end abolished the job. What waited at home was not danger, which he was good at, but conclusion: a farm, an audit, a marriage to resume, a son to meet, and no further chapters — the state the tradition calls nostos and this file calls the end of the story.

The theory: the ten years were not obstacles between the hero and home. They were the hero, generating obstacles, because a man who is only himself in transit will subcontract any monster, goddess, or weather system available to remain in transit. The gods' interference was real but oversubscribed — Poseidon supplied the storms, and the subject supplied the itinerary that kept sailing into them.

The 43 days

Alone among the theories, this file volunteers to absorb the unaccounted days — the 43-day residue in the Delay Attribution ledger that no monster or goddess claims. Its accounting: they were Tuesdays. Days of harbor-checking, rigging-inspection, wind-waiting, one-more-provisioning — the small, unnarratable frictions a reluctant traveler manufactures between himself and a finished journey. No bard sings the Tuesday. The theory holds that the decade was made of them, and the monsters were the exceptions.

Problems

  • It is unfalsifiable, and knows it. Any evidence of delay confirms it; any evidence of hurry (the nine-day sprint to within sight of Ithaca) it files as "the exception that calibrates the rule." The Committee's methodologists have lodged a standing objection, from which they keep requesting extensions.
  • The ending argues back. He did, eventually, go home — through the door, past the suitors, to the tree-bed and the twenty-year conversation. A pure procrastination theory must explain why the avoidance stopped, and its best answer ("he ran out of Mediterranean") is not its proudest.
  • Survives-Penelope score: 3. Surprisingly high. The Penelope proxy panel's finding: she'd suspected as much since the horse.

The Home Renovation Avoidance Hypothesis

"They slaughter our oxen and sheep and fat goats, and feast, and drink our sparkling wine recklessly — and the bulk of our wealth is wasted."
— Telemachus, describing what is, structurally, an unsupervised open-plan renovation with catering

Status: Contested. Survives-Penelope score: 0 (a rubric first). Claims the entire 3,650 days.

The Theory

By year three of the subject's absence, the palace on Ithaca hosted 108 suitors in permanent residence — eating the estate, courting Penelope, and generating precisely the domestic environment (crowded, loud, every decision contested by dozens of stakeholders) that the theory alleges the subject could sense from across the Mediterranean, the way sailors sense weather.

The hypothesis: the delay was avoidance. Not of Penelope — of the situation: the estate audit, the suitor-eviction project, the twenty years of deferred maintenance, and above all the conversations. A man who can talk his way past a cyclops can forecast, with professional accuracy, exactly how many consecutive evenings of stakeholder alignment await him at home. The theory holds that Ogygia was not a prison (The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation agrees) but a home office.

The loom question

The theory's most contested annex concerns the shroud. Penelope's documented weave-by-day, unweave-by-night protocol delayed the suitors three full years. The standard reading: a loyalty stratagem. The hypothesis's reading: a change-order system — the only party in the house generating deliverables was also the only party un-generating them nightly, which any project auditor recognizes as schedule control, and which kept the household in a state of permanent, deliberate incompletion that no returning owner could be blamed for.

The Committee declines to rule (counsel is retained; see Glossary#the-loom-question) but notes, quietly, that the household's two principals spent twenty years running the same strategy in opposite directions: one delaying a homecoming, one delaying a wedding, both via elaborate, deniable process.

Supporting Evidence

  • The disguise. On finally reaching Ithaca, the subject did not walk in the front door. He entered as a beggar, audited the household incognito for days, tested the staff, inventoried the suitors — an inspection protocol executed with more planning than any navigation decision of the preceding decade.
  • The bed test. Penelope's verification question — could the marriage bed be moved? — works only because the bed is built into a living olive tree, i.e., the house was constructed around a piece of furniture that can never be renovated. The Committee submits that no marriage anchored to structurally load-bearing furniture is casual about renovations, and rests.
  • The final delay was also his. Even after the suitors were dealt with, the subject's first full day home included: a fake wedding feast (his idea), an estate visit to his father (understandable), and a fresh armed standoff with the suitors' families (inevitable). The record contains no day on which he simply sat down.

Problems

  • It reverses the arrow of causation. The suitors occupied the palace because he was absent. A theory in which he stayed away to avoid the mess his staying away created is either circular or the single most honest description of procrastination ever filed — the Committee's plenary session on this question ended without quorum when several members left to "think about it."
  • Survives-Penelope score: 0. The hypothesis has never been stated in full within a stadion of Penelope. A junior clerk once began; the resulting document is in the Evidence Locker under exhibits, scorched, adjacent to the Sirens material.

The Lotus-Eaters Wellness Retreat

"Whoever ate the honey-sweet fruit of the lotus no longer wished to bring back word, nor to return."
— The official account, describing what the brochure calls "letting go of outcome-attachment"

Status: Active. Officially absorbs 9 days; the theory alleges up to 90.

The Theory

The land of the Lotus-Eaters was the ancient world's first all-inclusive wellness destination: a plant-based program, no fixed schedule, aggressively friendly locals, and a signature botanical experience that guests describe as "finally being present" and commanders describe as "desertion."

The official account says the stop was brief: three scouts sampled the program, stopped caring about the mission, and were dragged back to the ships in restraints. The theory's question is simple: who was running the stopwatch? The only timekeeper was Odysseus — a Class C source (see source classes) with a documented incentive to compress every comfortable stop and expand every monster.

The duration problem

The Committee's forensic reading finds the "nine days of storms, one day among the Lotus-Eaters" arithmetic suspicious on three grounds:

  1. Restraint logistics. Three grown oarsmen, restrained and stowed, implies rope, supervision rotas, and a cooling-off period. Fleet manuals of the era budget two weeks for a single deserter. The account budgets an afternoon for three.
  2. The fleet's mood. Immediately after departure, the fleet sails straight into the Polyphemus incident — a risk profile consistent with a crew coming off a mandatory-return deadline, not a rested one. See The 'Nobody' Personal Brand Crisis.
  3. Nobody counts storm days. The "nine days of storms" that precede the stop are unwitnessed and unfalsifiable. Storm days are where itineraries hide their unaccounted days — the Committee's full audit is at Delay Attribution.

Supporting Evidence

  • The lotus program's core promise — cease to wish for return — is precisely the outcome this entire wiki investigates. It would be professionally negligent not to ask whether the commander sampled the product.
  • The subject's telling of the episode is four sentences long. Four sentences, from a narrator who spends forty lines on a whirlpool. Brevity, in this source, is a tell.
  • No Lotus-Eater has ever been interviewed. Not because they refuse — because every envoy the Committee sends stays. The Committee has lost three interns and now conducts all lotus-related research by letter.

Problems

  • It's still small. Even the aggressive 90-day reading absorbs 2.5% of the missing decade. This theory is a warm-up act for The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation.
  • The restraints are corroborated. Rope purchases appear in the fleet's manifest (Evidence Locker). Genuine coercion was applied to someone — the theory must concede the program wasn't universally voluntary.

The 'Nobody' Personal Brand Crisis

"Cyclops, if anyone asks who blinded you, say it was Odysseus, sacker of cities, son of Laertes, whose home is on Ithaca!"
— The subject, shouting his full legal name, patronymic, and home address across open water, at the son of the sea god

Status: Active, strong. Absorbs most of the decade indirectly.

The Theory

The Polyphemus operation was, tactically, perfect. Infiltration, intoxication, the "Nobody" alias, the sheep-based exfiltration — flawless. Post-incident, the tradition tells us, Odysseus then delivered his name, lineage, and street address to the victim, whose father (Poseidon) ran the only road home.

The theory holds that everything after that shout is best understood as reputation management. You cannot sail home as the most famous cyclops-blinder in the Mediterranean while the ocean's owner has your name on a grudge. You can wait it out. The subject's decade — viewed through this lens — is a news cycle being aggressively outlasted from a series of undisclosed locations, several of them with excellent catering (The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation, The Aeaean Culinary Residency).

The escalation ladder

The Committee reconstructs the brand incident in four steps:

Step Action Brand impact
1 "My name is Nobody" Genius. Untraceable. The alias still taught in schools
2 Escape succeeds, fleet clears the harbor Clean exit. Story dies in one port
3 First taunt, from the ship Risky. Boulder narrowly misses. Story survives
4 Full name, patronymic, home address Poseidon files the grudge. Story acquires a mailing address

Steps 1–2 are the work of the cleverest man alive. Steps 3–4 are the work of a man who needs the review to be attributed. The theory's core insight: those are the same man, and the second one set the itinerary.

Supporting Evidence

  • The wrath is documented and name-indexed. Poseidon's interference is the official explanation for nearly every storm in the record. No name shouted, no wrath filed. The delay is causally downstream of a branding decision.
  • Every stop includes a name-drop audit. At each port, the subject either conceals his name (Phaeacia, initially; Ithaca, extensively) or performs it (The Phaeacian Speaking Tour). The record reads like a man A/B testing his own fame.
  • The beggar disguise. He enters his own house anonymously. The final mile of a ten-year journey is executed under an alias. This is not a man delayed by monsters; this is a man who never stopped managing the rollout.

Problems

  • Indirect attribution. The theory explains why the road was hostile, not why the layovers were seven years long. It requires a hospitality theory as a co-conspirator, which the Committee finds suspiciously convenient for the hospitality theories.
  • Motive duplication. The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination absorbs this theory as "Exhibit A: fear of arriving as himself." The two files cite each other in a loop the Committee has stopped trying to untangle.

The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation

"She keeps him captive there, and he weeps, sitting on the shore."
— Official account, of a man living rent-free in a goddess's beach cave with unlimited catering

Status: Leading theory. Absorbs 2,555 of the 3,650 missing days (70%).

The Theory

Calypso did not imprison Odysseus on Ogygia for seven years. She gave him the island tour, comped his stay, and opened with a genuinely strong offer — immortality, plus the property — and he did what every guest of a timeshare presentation does: he said "I just need to think about it" for seven consecutive years while accepting the free amenities.

The theory holds that the delay ended not through divine intervention but through the standard mechanism: the offer expired. Hermes' visit in year seven was not a rescue; it was the closing department flying in a senior negotiator, discovering the prospect would never sign, and cutting losses. Calypso's subsequent speed in supplying an axe, a raft, provisions, and favorable winds — within four days — is exactly the speed of an operation that has already moved on to the next prospect.

Supporting Evidence

  1. The amenities were accepted. Seven years of meals, lodging, and companionship. The official account's daily beach-weeping (attested only by the subject, see source classes) is consistent with buyer's-remorse-in-advance, a condition well documented among timeshare prospects.
  2. The offer on the table was immortality. No one deliberates seven years over an offer they intend to refuse. One deliberates seven years over an offer one intends to keep receiving.
  3. The exit logistics were instant. Four days from "you may go" to a fully provisioned raft. Compare: two months to leave Aeolus' floating island with a following wind (The Aeolus Baggage Incident). Ogygia had off-boarding procedures.
  4. No second prospect ever reported. Ogygia does not appear on any other itinerary in the ancient record. A one-prospect pipeline is consistent with a boutique operation — or, as the Committee speculates, one whose only listing was the presenter herself.

Problems

  • The captivity language. The tradition insists he was "held." The Committee notes that the holder had one (1) employee, no walls, and a raft's worth of timber growing on-site the entire time. The thing holding him was the catering.
  • The crying. Even hostile readings concede genuine shore-weeping. Timeshare prospects do cry, but rarely daily. Partial credit to the psychological theories (The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination).
  • Survives-Penelope score: 2. "I spent seven years deciding whether to become immortal with someone else" survives the conversation only slightly better than the renovation theory (The Home Renovation Avoidance Hypothesis), and only because Penelope respects a negotiator.

The Phaeacian Speaking Tour

"Why do you weep? — Tell us your story."
— King Alcinous, issuing history's most consequential speaker invitation

Status: Confirmed as an event; disputed as a theory. Absorbs only 3 days — but produces the source for all the others.

The Theory

The stop at Scheria was short. Its significance is not the delay; it is that everything this Inquiry investigates was first performed there.

Wrecked, anonymous, and rescued by the princess Nausicaa, the subject was hosted at the Phaeacian court under premium xenia. When the court bard performed Troy material and the anonymous guest wept at his own scenes, the king asked for his story. What followed — Lotus-Eaters, Polyphemus, Aeolus, Circe, the Underworld, Sirens, the strait, the cattle, Calypso — is a four-book keynote, delivered in one evening, by a professional, to an audience whose applause was denominated in cargo.

The Phaeacians paid the speaker in bronze, gold, textiles, and a chauffeured overnight boat to Ithaca. The Committee restates that plainly: the ten-year itinerary, as we have it, is the transcript of a gig whose compensation scaled with how good the story was.

Supporting Evidence

  • The incentive gradient is on the page. Mid-performance, the subject pauses; the king responds by pledging more gifts and asking for more; the performance resumes, longer. The record preserves its own pay-per-episode mechanics.
  • The teller controls all Class C material. Every uncorroborated marvel in the Theory Catalog — every monster no witness survived, every year no log covers — enters the tradition through this single evening. See source classes: Books 9–12 are Class C by construction.
  • He had form. Weeping at the bard's Troy set, then requesting more Troy — the appetite documented at The Sirens' Encore Clause — immediately precedes the keynote. The man warmed up his own material.
  • The boat home took one night. After ten years, the actual remaining distance was one (1) chauffeured overnight sail. The Committee has read this sentence many times and it does not improve.

The Sirens' Encore Clause

"Untie me! — and he commanded them with his brows, but they bent to their oars and rowed on."
— The record. He asked. To stay. At the Sirens.

Status: Active, tiny, philosophically load-bearing. Absorbs 1 day.

The Theory

The standard reading of the Sirens episode is risk management: wax for the crew, rope for the captain, science achieved safely. The theory before the Committee reads the same facts as a concert booking.

Consider what the subject actually arranged: the only man aboard permitted to hear the performance, in a dedicated seat (the mast), with a contractual guarantee that his requests for an encore be ignored. He did not neutralize the Sirens. He engineered the world's safest front-row ticket and made his crew the venue security.

And when the music played, the record is explicit: he demanded to stay. The rope is the only reason this wiki's subject line isn't "The Sirens' Permanent Residency."

What the Sirens actually sing

The detail the tradition undersells: the Sirens' setlist is not seduction in the abstract. They sing "everything that happened at Troy" — they offer the listener his own story, told perfectly, forever.

The theory's central claim follows: the Sirens are the pure form of every delay in this Inquiry. Calypso offered him immortality; Circe offered him comfort; Scheria offered him an audience (The Phaeacian Speaking Tour); the Sirens offered him himself, as content. He demanded the encore. He always demands the encore. The other eleven files in the Theory Catalog are this file with better catering.

Supporting Evidence

  • The staging is his. Circe provided the warning and the wax protocol. The tied-to-the-mast-hearing arrangement — the part that converts a hazard into a ticket — was the subject's own amendment.
  • The request to stay is on the record. Preserved in his own telling. Even the subject could not resist reporting that he could not resist. (Class C source behaving exactly per profile.)
  • Precedent. He wept at the Phaeacian court when the bard sang of Troy — then commissioned more Troy material (The Phaeacian Speaking Tour). A man who tips the orchestra for the song that makes him cry has a relationship with his own myth that this Committee is not qualified to bill for.

Problems

  • One day. The theory absorbs a single afternoon. As a delay explanation it is negligible; as a diagnosis it is the skeleton key to the whole file cabinet, which is why The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination cites it fourteen times.
  • Survives-Penelope score: 1. "I arranged to be tied up so I could safely listen to two women sing about how great I am" has been read aloud to a Penelope proxy panel. The panel's ruling is preserved in the Evidence Locker under exhibits, scorched.

The Strait Insurance Dispute

"Six of my company, the best in strength and hands, Scylla snatched from the hollow ship."
— The incident report. Note the phrasing: not "we lost six men" but "Scylla snatched" — liability assigned in the same breath as the casualty count.

Status: Speculative. Alleges 60–90 days of administrative delay. Textual support: thin and the Committee knows it.

The Theory

The passage between Scylla and Charybdis was a known-hazard transit, briefed in advance by a qualified pilot (Circe), with a documented expected-loss figure: six men. The subject accepted the routing, chose not to brief the crew on the expected-loss clause (a management pattern the Committee has flagged before — see the baggage incident), and armed himself against instructions for a fight the briefing had explicitly ruled unwinnable.

Six men were lost, per the estimate. The theory holds that what followed — in any fleet with a benefits structure — was a liability review: who accepted the routing, who knew the expected-loss figure, why the deceased were rowing (unbriefed) at stations closest to the cliff, and whether "the pilot said six" constitutes informed consent when only one person heard the pilot.

Maritime tribunals of the era, per the Committee's actuarial consultants, budget ten to fifteen days per casualty for a contested finding. Six casualties: 60–90 days. The theory parks those days in the unlogged stretch before Thrinacia — where, it notes, the fleet then lingered a full month (The Thrinacian Catering Invoice), exactly as a fleet does when its paperwork has not cleared.

Supporting Evidence

  • The expected-loss figure existed in writing. Circe's routing advice is quoted verbatim in the record: you will lose six. No other delay in the file has a pre-registered casualty forecast. This is what makes it an insurance matter rather than a tragedy: someone had the number in advance.
  • The armor discrepancy. The pilot's brief: do not fight, row fast. The subject's action: full armor, two spears, posted on the foredeck. The brief was violated by the reviewing officer, which in every era known to the Committee converts a loss into a dispute.
  • The subject's account spends its length on the monster and a single sentence on the aftermath. Class C compression behavior (source profile): expand the monster, compress the meeting.

Problems

  • No tribunal appears in the text. None. The Committee's own rubric scores this a 1 and the theory's sponsor has accepted the score "under protest, pending discovery of the minutes."
  • The crew's benefits structure is conjectural. The era's rowers were compensated in shares of plunder and glory; the theory requires at least one grieving family with a claims process, and the record supplies only the grieving.
  • Simpler competitor. The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination explains the same lingering with no tribunal at all: after watching six men eaten, nobody wanted to get back in the boat, and the man setting the schedule wanted it least of all.

The Thrinacian Catering Invoice

"For a full month the South Wind blew without ceasing, and there was no other wind."
— The official account of why a fleet sat for thirty days next to the one herd of cattle in the world it had been told not to eat

Status: Active. Absorbs 30 documented days plus the terminal storm.

The Theory

The stop at Thrinacia — the Sun's island, home of the untouchable cattle — was provisioning theatre. The fleet had been warned twice (Tiresias, then Circe) with the century's clearest compliance directive: whatever else you do, do not eat the cows. The subject's response was to anchor next to the cows, for a month, with dwindling supplies, on the strength of a wind report.

The theory does not dispute the wind. It disputes the menu planning. A commander who knows (a) the supplies, (b) the crew, and (c) the exact nature of the one catastrophic temptation on the island, and who then allows the siege of hunger to run thirty days, has not been trapped by weather. He has constructed a compliance failure and assigned it to meteorology.

The invoice

The record preserves what the Committee regards as the era's most damning expense document. When the crew (led by Eurylochus, whose Redacted Testimony remains lost) finally slaughtered the cattle, they attempted to regularize the transaction:

Line item Standard practice What the crew had Substitution filed
Barley scattering (rite) Sacred barley None (eaten weeks prior) Oak leaves
Wine libation (rite) Wine None (drunk weeks prior) Water
The cattle themselves Purchasable stock The Sun's personal herd A promise to build a temple later

Substituting oak leaves for barley in a sacrifice is, in accounting terms, photocopying a receipt you drew yourself. Helios escalated directly to Zeus. The fleet was written off in the subsequent storm — total loss, one survivor, who floated to Ogygia on the keel and into The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation.

Supporting Evidence

  • The month is undisputed. Thirty days on the record, corroborated by the wind pattern. This is the third-largest admitted block in the Delay Attribution ledger.
  • The compliance directive was written and repeated. Two independent prophetic sources, same clause. The subject's own account confirms receipt. There is no ambiguity defense.
  • The oath he extracted was unenforceable. He had the crew swear not to touch the cattle — then took the only reliable enforcement officer (himself) inland for a nap of unrecorded length. The Committee has audited many delegation failures; this one is taught.

Problems

  • The hunger was real. Twenty days without provisions before the slaughter. The Committee grades excuses, and "we were starving next to a buffet we were forbidden to touch" is — uniquely in this file — an excuse with calories.
  • The delay is bounded. Thirty days plus a storm. The theory is solid but cannot scale; its real contribution is establishing the compliance-failure-by-design pattern that The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination cites as Exhibit D.

The Underworld Networking Summit

"I would have seen the men of former times, whom I wished to see —"
— The subject, mid-Underworld, explicitly extending his own agenda

Status: Active, small but suspicious. Officially 1 day. The Committee's read: up to 30.

The Theory

The trip to the Underworld had one (1) deliverable: consult the prophet Tiresias about the route home. Tiresias delivers the consult early in the session — route, hazards, the cattle warning (The Thrinacian Catering Invoice), the odd bit about the inland oar. Deliverable complete.

The subject then stays. He works the room. The record — his own record, mind — lists the meetings: his mother; Agamemnon (a cautionary tale about homecomings, filed and ignored); Achilles (an exit interview on glory, negative); Ajax (declined the meeting, still bitter — see the polytropos problem); Heracles; Minos; a parade of famous women the account catalogues by name for over fifty lines. This is not an errand. This is a summit with a keynote, and the subject attended every breakout session.

The agenda, reconstructed

Session Attendee Deliverable?
Route consultation Tiresias Yes — the only one
Family reunion Anticleia (mother) No (understandable)
"Lessons in Homecoming" Agamemnon No (ignored anyway)
"Glory: A Retrospective" Achilles No (conclusions rejected)
Attempted reconciliation Ajax No (meeting declined)
Celebrity observation Heracles, Minos, Tityos, Sisyphus, Tantalus No
The Catalogue of Famous Women ~14 named No, and the Committee has questions about the runtime

Supporting Evidence

  • The session count. No one-day event has this agenda. Conference planners consulted by the Committee estimate the reconstructed agenda at "three days minimum, a week with corridor conversations, a month if there was a poster session." The dead do not enforce checkout times.
  • The return leg went backwards. From the Underworld, the fleet sails back to Aeaea — a full return to the previous venue for "the burial of Elpenor" and one more night of Circe's catering (The Aeaean Culinary Residency). Burying Elpenor was correct. Dining Elpenor's memory into a second Aeaean stopover is where the audit flags it.
  • Elpenor himself. The crewman died by falling off Circe's roof because the departure was so abrupt he was left behind. The Committee notes the operational tempo: a year of feasting, then an exit so rushed it produced casualties. This is not a fleet that manages time. This is a fleet that manages momentum.

Problems

  • Chronology is genuinely hard below decks. The Underworld has no sun (Thrinacia's owner being unavailable there, of all places). Day-counting claims in either direction are unfalsifiable, which offends the Committee's whole apparatus.
  • The motive is sympathetic. He met his mother, who had died waiting for him. The Committee grades files, not grief, and grades this one gently.

Reading order

New contributors should read in evidence order, not scoreboard order:

  1. Start with the Timeline of the Ten Years so the numbers mean something.
  2. Read The Phaeacian Speaking Tour — it's the theory about the source of all the other theories, which is why its textual score is perfect and its trustworthiness is not.
  3. Then the two heavyweights: The Ogygia Timeshare Presentation (7 years) and The Aeaean Culinary Residency (1 year). Together they absorb 80% of the delay.
  4. Finish with The Grand Unified Theory of Heroic Procrastination, which claims all the others as special cases and has the citations to make it awkward.
